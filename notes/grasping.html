Grasping

Main Problems

  • Finding a feasible grasp on a given object
    • Object surface geometry representation
      • Parametric surface
      • Surface points and their normals
    • Finding the optimal grasp on a given object
      • Task based
      • Grasp evaluation (grasp metric)
    • Grasp classification
    • Determine if a grasp is force closure, given a set of contact points and contact model
    • Determine if the fingers are able to apply a specified resultant wrench on the object, given a grasp, joint torque limits, and hand kinematic structure.
    • Compute the optimal contact forces
  • Solving the inverse kinematics for a multi-fingered hand
    • Finding grasp configurations
      • Precision grasp
        • Local convergence methods (single solution, or fail if one exists)
      • Power grasp
    • Optimizing grasp configurations
    • Accounting for compliance
  • Searching a collision-free trajectory that brings the hand to the grasping pose
  • Grasping control

Power Grasp

♥ General formulation for force distribution in power grasp [Mirza, Orin, 1994]

Problem:

  • The formulation of the force distribution problem for precision grasps (with fingertips only) has been well studied, but little research has been done on power grasps.
  • Determination of joint torques for optimum force distribution in power grasps.

Difficulties:

  • High degree of static indeterminacy due to multiplicity of contacts.
  • Friction constraints leads to a large number of slip mode permutations.
  • Evaluation of the stability properties of power grasps.

Methodology:

  • The force distribution equations formulated include the inertial effects of the fingers along with the physical limits on the actuator torques, using inverse dynamics and Jacobian relationships.
  • The formulation is then transformed to a constrained optimization problem and is represented in a concise matrix form which can be solved using the linear programming method.

Robotic Hand IK

♥ Calculating hand configurations for precision and pinch grasps [Borst et al., 2002]

Borst, Ch, Max Fischer, and Gerd Hirzinger. “Calculating hand configurations for precision and pinch grasps.“ IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems. Vol. 2. IEEE, 2002.

The grasp inverse kinematics problem is formualted as a set of unconstrained optimization problems where the contact and kinematic constraints and the joint limits are introduced as penalty terms in the objective function.

Precision grasp:

  • Only fingertip is in contact with the object
  • One contact per finger
  • Non-deformable object
  • Fingertip model
    • 3-DoF Universal Joint (spherical fingertip model), or
    • 1-DoF Revolute Joint

Pinch grasp:

  • Cylindrical fingertip model

    Reason: Humans use the pad of their fingertips to increase stability when they manipulate or pick up small and light object.

  • Cylinder axis is perpendicular to object surface normal

  • 2-DoF = Prismatic + Revolute along $Z_{\rm tip}$

Problem Formulation:

  • Each finger has four free parameters ($\theta_1,\theta_2, \theta_3, l$)

  • Equality constraints for the contact terms $h_i(\boldsymbol{x}) = 0$

    Corresponding penalty function:

  • Inequality constraints for the joint limits $g_j(\boldsymbol{x}) \leq 0$

    Corresponding penalty function:

  • Penalty function are chosen so that

    Parameter $p_k$ increases in each step.

  • Optimization problem

    Solved by Mathematica builtin Levenberg-Marquardt minimizer.

Pipeline:

  1. Choose contact points randomly on the object
  2. Calculate a kinematically valid hand configuration using an optimization approach; Start at (1) if there is none.
  3. Perform a collision test to see if geometric constraints fail; Start at (1) in case of a collision.
  4. Compute a quality measure for the grasp.
  5. Store the grasp in a list sorted by the grasp quality and start at (1) until there are enough grasps in the list or a given time limit is exceeded.

Prediction of fingers posture using artificial neural networks [Rezzoug, Gorce, 2008]

Rezzoug, Nasser, and Philippe Gorce. “Prediction of fingers posture using artificial neural networks.” Journal of biomechanics 41.12 (2008): 2743-2749.

In this paper, a technique based on neural networks is proposed to learn the inverse kinematics mapping between the fingertip 3D position and the corresponding joint angles. Finger movements are obtained by an instrumented glove and are mapped to a multichain model of the hand. From the fingertip desired position, the neural networks allow predicting the corresponding finger joint angles keeping the specific subject coordination patterns. Two sets of movements are considered in this study. The first one, the training set, consisting of free fingers movements is used to construct the mapping between fingertip position and joint angles. The second one, constructed for testing purposes, is composed of a sequence of grasping tasks of everyday-life objects. The maximal mean error between fingertip measured position and fingertip position obtained from simulated joint angles and forward kinematics is 0.99±0.76 mm for the training set and 1.49±1.62 mm for the test set. Also, the maximal RMS error of joint angles prediction is 2.85° and 5.10° for the training and test sets respectively, while the maximal mean joint angles prediction error is −0.11±4.34° and −2.52±6.71° for the training and test sets, respectively. Results relative to the learning and generalization capabilities of this architecture are also presented and discussed.

Define: Grasp

Combination of individual primitive contacts (hard/soft point contacts with/without friction).

Any complex contact can be described as a convex sum of the primitive contacts:

  • Edge contact without friction

    Convex sum of two frictionless contact points (at one end of the edge contact)

  • Face contact

    Convex sum of three contacts at the vertices

Theorem: Vector Closure (Goldman & Tucker)

In an $n$-dimensional vector space, a set of vectos $V$ is vector-closure if and only if $V$ has at least $n+1$ vectos, such that

  1. $n$ of the $n+1$ vectors are linearly independent.

  2. A strictly positive combination of the $n+1$ vectos is the zeros vector.

    $$ \sum_{i=1}^{n+1} \alpha_i \boldsymbol{v}_i = \boldsymbol{0}, \quad \alpha_i > 0.$$

1. Force direction closure

Theorem: Force-direction Closure

A set of plannar wrenches $W$ can generate force in any direction iff there exists a three-tuple of wrenches $(\wb_1, \wb_2, \wb_3)$ whose respective force directions $\fb_1, \fb_2, \fb_3$ satisfy

  1. Two of the three directions $\fb_1, \fb_2, \fb_3$ are independent.
  2. A strictly positive combination of the three directions is zero: $\alpha \fb_1 + \beta \fb_2 + \gamma \fb_3 = \boldsymbol{0}$.

2. Torque closure

Achieve torque closure with only pure forces.

Theorem: Torque Closure

A set of plannar wrenches $W$ can generate clockwise and counterclockwise torques iff there exists a four-tuple of forces $(\wb_1, \wb_2, \wb_3, \wb_4)$ such taht the following hold

  1. Three of the four forces have lines of action that do not intersect at a common point or at infinity.

  2. Lef $\fb_1, \cdots, \fb_4$ be the force directions of $\wb_1, \cdots, \wb_4$. Let $\pb_{12}$ be the point where the lines of action of $\wb_1$ and $\wb_2$ intersect. There exists $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta > 0$ such that

    $$\pb_{34} - \pb_{12} = \pm(\alpha \fb_1 + \beta \fb_2) = \mp (\gamma \fb_3 + \delta \fb_4).$$

Determining force-closure grasps reachable by a given hand [Gilart, Suárez, 2012]

Gilart, Fidel, and Raúl Suárez. “Determining force-closure grasps reachable by a given hand.” IFAC Proceedings Volumes 45.22 (2012): 235-240.

The paper presents an approach to find contact points on an object surface that are reachable by a given hand and such that the resulting grasp satisfies the force-closure condition. This is a very common problem that still requires a practical solution. The proposed method is based on the computation of a set of independent contact regions on the object boundary such that a finger contact on each region produce a force-closure grasp, and then this set of regions is iteratively recomputed while looking for a set of contact points that are reachable by a given hand. The search is done guided by a cost function that indicates the proximity of the hand fingertips to a candidate set of grasping contact points.

Planning for Grasp and Hand Configuration

♥ A Criteria-Based Approach to Grasp Synthesis [Hester, et al., 1999]

Hester, R. D., et al. “A criteria-based approach to grasp synthesis.” Proceedings 1999 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (Cat. No. 99CH36288C). Vol. 2. IEEE, 1999.

A synthesis approach has been introduced for grasp planning problem of robotic hands. The approach tries to decompose the synthesis problem into two interconnected problems in finger and hand levels, preliminary and final grasp, respectively. At each level, a grasp configuration is selected using different performance criteria. In the case of preliminary grasp, the selected configuration is fed into the final grasp phase, which constitutes a starting point for the grasp optimization. The finger and hand level criteria are connected, so the two-phase approach is justified.

Settings:

  • Limited to precision grasp
  • No rollinga and sliding occurs at the contact point

Methodology:

  • Preliminary Grasp
  • Grasp Optimization

Planning for Grasp Trajectory

♥ Integrated grasp and motion planning [Vahrenkamp et al., 2010]

Vahrenkamp, Nikolaus, et al. “Integrated grasp and motion planning.” 2010 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation. IEEE, 2010.
N. Vahrenkamp, T. Asfour and R. Dillmann, “Simultaneous Grasp and Motion Planning: Humanoid Robot ARMAR-III,” in IEEE Robotics & Automation Magazine, vol. 19, no. 2, pp. 43-57, June 2012.

Problems:

  • Finding a feasible grasp
  • Solving the inverse kinematics
  • Searching a collision-free trajectory that brings the hand to the grasping pose

Contributions:

  • The Grasp–RRT algorithm is able to find suitable solutions quickly while having the advantage that no IK solver, no reachability information, and no dedicated grasp planner
    must be present for the robot.

Settings:

  • No explicit target configuration
  • An anthropomorphic hand driven by a 6-DOF arm

Methodology:

  • Combines the search for a collision-free motion with the online search for a feasible grasp.
  • Hand = grasping center point (GCP) + approach direction (mandually defined).
  • Hand State = palm’s pose (SE3) + joint configuration
  • Move hand towards the object, then close the fingers.
    • Let the arm drive the hand towards the object via a RRT based planning approach. Move towards the nearest point on the object surface. Move a small step each iteration through the arm’s inverse kinematics.
    • Closing the hand is performed by iteratively moving the finger joints with small steps until (self-)collisions are detected.
    • Evaluate the grasp quality based on a certain metric.
  • To let the hand uniformly approach the object:
    • An ApproachSphere sphere is used to encode the distribution of the nodes on the RRT.
    • An ApproachSphere is a sphere that is located at the object’s center of mass.
    • A newly generated node will be projected onto the surface of the ApproachSphere.

Planning for Finger Gaits

Finger Gaits Planning for Multifingered Manipulation [Xu, Koo, Li, 2007]

Xu, Jijie, T. John Koo, and Zexiang Li. “Finger gaits planning for multifingered manipulation.“ 2007 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems. IEEE, 2007.

A robotic hand may change its grasp status and relocate some of its fingers in order to perform a large scale manipulation. Such a strategy is called a finger gait. In this paper, a randomized manipulation planning algorithm is proposed to solving the finger gait planning problem. One of the most used finger gaiting primitives, finger substitution, is introduced. Because of its discrete-continuous characteristics, the kinematics model of a finger substitution is formulated into a hybrid automaton. Considering the discrete and continuous topology of the automaton, both the discrete metric and continuous metric are defined on the state space. An improved RRT based planner is proposed to find a feasible finger substitution. Finally, simulation results verify the validity of the proposed finger gait planner.

Why soft or under-actuated hands?

  • Fingers can adaptively reconfigure when external force are applied
  • Do not require force or tactile sensing
  • Convert force control to motion control
  • Enables integrated grasp and motion planning
  • Allows violation of the environment constraints (Touching depth tolerance)

Start and goal configurations

  • The start-goal pair determines whether the object graspable in the end
  • Many pairs can not be connected by an arm motion due to the environment constraints
  • Inefficient to sample and check before motion planning

Sample-able Regions for an Integrated Solution

  1. Sampling start and goral configuration
  2. Connecting the two sample forests bidirectionally
  3. Projecting configurations to the constraint manifold
  4. Validating the connected pairs once connected
