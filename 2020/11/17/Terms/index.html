Robotic Terms

Yuechuan's Blog
2020-11-17
robotics
Term Description
Source
Finger gait A periodic sequence of finger relocations. Hong et al., 1990
Regrasping The object is released and regrouped in order to change its position and orientation within the grasp. Ma & Dollar, 2011
In-Grasp manipulation This type of manipulation utilizes the kinematic redundancy of fingers to make small changes to the object’s orientation and position while maintaining fingertip contact with the object. Ma & Dollar, 2011
Form closure Assume that a hand grasping an object has its joint angles locked and its palm fixed in space; then the grasp has form closure, or the object is form closed, if it is impossible to move the object, even infinitesimally. Handbook of Robotics, 2008
Force closure A grasp on an object is force-closure if it can exert an arbitrary force and torque on the object through the set of contacts. Hong et al., 1990
If for any noncontact wrench experienced by the object, contact wrench intensities exist that satisfy (28.20) and are consistent with the constraints imposed by the friction models applicable at the contact points. Handbook of Robotics, 2008
Antipodal points Two points on the boundary of a planar object are called antipodal if the positive ray through each point in the direction of the inward normal contains the other point Hong et al., 1990
Power grasp A grasp that the its mechanism can resist passively against upper-bound external force in whole direction without relying on feedback control of torques. Zhang et al., 1994
Power grasps are distinguished by large areas of contact between the grasped object and the surfaces of the fingers and palm and by little or no ability to impart motions with the fingers. Cutkosky, 1989
When some feasible joint torques have been assigned to finger joints in advance, the power grasp can automatically change its contact forces to resist magnitude-bounded external forces acting on the object from any direction without changing the preloaded joint torques. Yu et al., 1998
A power grasp is characterized by multiple points of contact between the object grasped and the surfaces of the fingers and palm. It maximizes the load carrying capability of a grasping system and is inherently highly stable because of the enveloping nature of the grasp which provides form-closure. Mirza & Orin, 1994
The fingers apply forces against the palm to restrain the object.
Kinematic manipulation Kinematic manipulation can be analyzed using only kinematics, and every motion of the manipulated object can be obtained by the knowledge of the movements of the robotic hand. An example might be the programming of a pick-and-place operation based on a purely kinematic representation of the task. Ruggiero et al., 2018
Static manipulation Static manipulation is instead studied through both kinematics and static forces. An example might be the object grasped by a parallel-jaw gripper where frictional forces are required to verify stability. Ruggiero et al., 2018
Quasi-static manipulation Quasi-static manipulation makes use of kinematics, static and quasi-static forces, like frictional forces in sliding contacts. An illustrative example might be pushing an object on a table. Ruggiero et al., 2018
Dynamic manipulation Within dynamic manipulation, forces due to accelerations play a relevant role along with kinematics, static and quasi-static forces. A simple example is the balance of a rolling object on the palm of the hand. It is thus apparent that each level of the taxonomy encompasses the previous one. Hence, a dynamic manipulation task deals also with kinematic, static and quasi-static sub-problems. Ruggiero et al., 2018
Nonprehensile manipulation A manipulation task is said to be nonprehensile if the object is subject only to unilateral constraints. Ruggiero et al., 2018
